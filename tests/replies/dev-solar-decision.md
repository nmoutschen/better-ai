<!-- prompt: Should I buy solar panels or a battery first for a house with a south-facing roof? -->
**Panels first.** A battery without generation just stores grid power, which only pays off with big time-of-use price gaps. Panels produce the savings on their own, and a south-facing roof is the ideal orientation (in the northern hemisphere), so you'll get strong yield.

**Why panels first:**
- Panels have the better payback, typically several years shorter than a battery.
- A battery's value depends on having surplus solar to store, so it makes sense only after you know your generation and usage patterns.
- You can add a battery later. Many inverters are "battery-ready" or hybrid, so ask your installer for one if you think you'll add storage.

**When a battery first could make sense:**
- You have steep time-of-use tariffs (cheap night rates, expensive peak rates) and want to arbitrage.
- You need backup power for outages.
- Your utility pays very little for exported solar (low or no feed-in tariff), so self-consumption matters most.

In that last case, still buy panels first, but plan the battery soon after.

**Practical tips:**
- Size the panel system to your annual usage, and a bit above if you plan an EV or heat pump.
- Choose a hybrid inverter or confirm AC/DC-coupled battery compatibility up front.
- Check local incentives, since some tax credits or rebates cover batteries only when paired with solar or installed at the same time. Where that applies, installing together can be cheaper than doing it separately.
