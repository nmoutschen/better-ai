<!-- prompt: I have a job offer with a lower salary but equity and a shorter commute. How should I weigh it? -->
Put a dollar figure on each side, then ask whether you could live with the equity being worth zero.

**1. Salary gap (certain)**
- Compare after-tax, not gross. Include bonus, 401k/pension match, and benefits.
- Annualize it. A €10k/year gap is €40k over four years.

**2. Commute (certain, and underrated)**
- Hours saved per week × 48 weeks. At 5 hours a week, that's about 240 hours a year, or six work weeks.
- Add direct costs (fuel, transit, parking, wear on the car).
- Research on well-being consistently finds long commutes to be one of the things people adapt to least. Weight this more than the spreadsheet suggests.

**3. Equity (uncertain, so discount it heavily)**
Ask the company:
- Type: options or RSUs? What's the strike price and the latest 409A or valuation?
- Your percentage of fully diluted shares, and the vesting schedule (cliff, 4 years?).
- Exercise window after you leave (90 days is a trap for many people).
- Preferences ahead of common stock, and how much funding remains.
- Is there any liquidity path (IPO, tender offers, acquisition)?

If it's a private startup, a reasonable default is to value the equity at 0-10% of its paper value. Treat any payout as upside, not income. If it's RSUs in a public company, it's nearly cash, so count about 80-100% of its value, minus the risk of the stock dropping.

**4. Non-financial factors**
- Learning, scope, and the quality of the manager and team.
- Job stability and how the company is doing.
- How the role affects your next move.

**A quick test**
Take the salary gap plus the commute cost. Would you take the new job if the equity ended up worth nothing? If yes, the equity is a free bonus and the decision is easy. If no, you're paying real money for a lottery ticket, so only do it if the equity terms are strong and you can afford the lower pay.

**Also consider**
- Your finances: can you absorb the lower pay without stress (rent, dependents, debt, savings)?
- Negotiation: companies often move on base, or on equity size and exercise window, if you ask.
