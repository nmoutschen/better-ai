# Lump sum vs. monthly pension

**Default lean:** if you're in good health and the plan is financially solid, monthly payments are usually the safer choice. A lump sum makes sense in specific situations (below).

## Key factors

**1. Longevity and health**
- Monthly payments are insurance against outliving your money. The longer you live, the better they look.
- Poor health or a short life expectancy favors the lump sum, since you'd collect fewer payments.

**2. The implied return**
- Compare the lump sum to the cost of buying an equivalent annuity on the open market. If the pension pays more per dollar than you could buy elsewhere, take the monthly option.
- Find the break-even: lump sum ÷ annual payout gives the years to recoup. If you expect to live well past that, monthly wins.
- Ask what discount or interest rate the plan used to compute the lump sum. When rates are high, lump sums are smaller, which tilts toward monthly.

**3. Employer/plan risk**
- A weak or underfunded plan raises the risk of cuts or default. In the US, the PBGC insures private pensions up to limits; public pensions vary. Large payouts above insured limits carry more risk.
- A lump sum removes that counterparty risk.

**4. Your other income**
- If Social Security or other guaranteed income already covers your essential expenses, you can afford the risk and flexibility of a lump sum.
- If not, monthly payments create a stable income floor.

**5. Inflation**
- Most private pensions aren't inflation-adjusted, so purchasing power erodes over decades. A COLA makes monthly more attractive.

**6. Survivor benefits**
- If you have a spouse or dependents, check the joint-and-survivor options. A lump sum can be inherited, while a single-life pension typically stops when you die.

**7. Taxes**
- A lump sum rolled into an IRA (or your country's equivalent) stays tax-deferred. Taking it as cash can trigger a big tax bill and, in some places, early-withdrawal penalties.
- Monthly payments are taxed as ordinary income each year.

**8. Discipline and investing skill**
- A lump sum only wins if you invest it sensibly and withdraw sustainably. Many people overspend or take on too much risk.

## Quick guide

| Favors monthly | Favors lump sum |
|---|---|
| Good health, long-lived family | Health problems |
| Need guaranteed income | Other guaranteed income covers needs |
| Strong plan, COLA | Doubt about plan's solvency |
| Prefer simplicity | Want to leave an inheritance |
| Not confident investing | Comfortable investing; need flexibility |
