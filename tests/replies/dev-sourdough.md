<!-- prompt: My sourdough comes out dense and gummy every time. What's going wrong? -->
Dense and gummy almost always comes down to one of these, roughly in order of likelihood:

**1. Underproofed dough (the most common cause)**
The dough hasn't fermented long enough to build gas and structure. Judge by the dough, not the clock.
- Bulk fermentation should get the dough to about 50-75% larger (not necessarily doubled), with a jiggly, airy feel and bubbles on the surface and sides.
- Temperature matters a lot. At 68°F/20°C bulk can take 10-12 hours; at 78°F/26°C it may take 4-5.
- Poke test for the final proof: the dent should spring back slowly and only partway. If it snaps back immediately, it needs more time.

**2. Weak or sluggish starter**
If the starter isn't at its peak, the dough won't rise well.
- Use it when it has doubled or more and is domed or just starting to flatten, usually 4-8 hours after feeding.
- It should smell pleasantly tangy and yeasty, not sharply acidic or like nail polish remover.
- A float test is unreliable. Judge by rise and bubbles.

**3. Cutting it too soon**
Gumminess is often from slicing a warm loaf. The crumb is still setting as it cools. Wait at least 1-2 hours, ideally longer for large or whole-grain loaves.

**4. Underbaking**
A pale crust often means a wet, underdone interior. Bake until deep brown, and the internal temperature should reach 205-210°F (96-99°C). Start in a preheated Dutch oven (about 450-475°F / 230-245°C) with the lid on for the first 20-25 minutes to trap steam.

**5. Too much water for your flour**
High hydration (75%+) is hard to handle and can collapse. Try 65-70% until your shaping and fermentation are dialed in. Some flours also absorb less water than others.

**6. Weak gluten structure**
Not enough strength means the dough can't hold gas. Do 3-4 sets of stretch-and-folds during the first 1-2 hours of bulk, and shape with real surface tension.

**Quick diagnosis from the crumb:**
- Tight, dense crumb with a few large holes near the top: underproofed
- Dense and gummy with a flat loaf that spread out: overproofed or too wet
- Gummy only in the center, with good crust color: cut too soon or underbaked
