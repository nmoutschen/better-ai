<!-- prompt: My adult dog suddenly started barking at night. What should I try? -->
A sudden change in an adult dog's behavior usually has a trigger, so start by working out what changed.

**1. Rule out health issues**
- Pain, arthritis, or digestive upset can make a dog restless and vocal at night.
- Needing to pee more often (urinary infection, kidney issues, diabetes) is another common cause.
- Hearing or vision loss can make a dog anxious in the dark. In older dogs, canine cognitive dysfunction (like dementia) often shows up as night waking and barking.
- If it's truly out of the blue, or you see other changes (appetite, thirst, accidents, pacing), book a vet visit.

**2. Look for environmental triggers**
- New noises outside, such as wildlife, neighbors, a new pet nearby, or fireworks.
- Changes at home: moved furniture, a new baby or partner, a new sleeping spot, or a different schedule.
- Temperature, or light coming through a window.

**3. Consider anxiety or unmet needs**
- Separation anxiety can appear suddenly after a routine change, a stressful event, or a move.
- Not enough exercise or mental stimulation during the day leaves energy that comes out at night.
- A late meal, or a last potty break that's too early.

**What to try**
- Add a last potty trip right before bed.
- Give a good walk or some sniffing or puzzle-toy time in the evening.
- Block the view or sound: close curtains, use white noise or a fan, or move the bed away from windows and doors.
- Keep the sleeping spot comfortable and close to you, if that calms them.
- When they bark, check once for a real need (potty, water, pain). If there isn't one, stay calm and boring, and don't reward the barking with play or treats.
- Reward quiet moments, such as a treat when they settle.

**Go to the vet sooner if** the barking comes with confusion, pacing, whining as if in pain, changes in drinking or urination, or if the dog is a senior.
