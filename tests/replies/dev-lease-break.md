<!-- prompt: Can I leave my apartment lease early if my employer moves me to another city? -->
Maybe. It depends on where you live and what your lease says. There's usually no automatic right to break a lease just because your employer relocates you, but you often have options.

**Where there may be a legal right**
- **Military orders:** In the US, the SCRA gives service members the right to terminate. This is the one nearly universal exception.
- **Some jurisdictions:** Certain countries and a few US states allow early termination for job relocation, often with a notice period of 1-3 months. In many European countries (France, Belgium, Germany in some cases), tenants can terminate with shortened notice for a job transfer. In most US states, there is no such right.

**What to check in your lease**
- **Early termination or buyout clause:** Many leases let you leave by giving 30-60 days' notice and paying a fee, often 1-2 months' rent.
- **Job relocation clause:** Some leases include one, especially in corporate or large-complex housing.
- **Sublet or assignment clause:** If allowed, this can end your liability, though the landlord usually has to approve the replacement.

**Practical options if you have no right to leave**
1. **Talk to your landlord early.** Many will accept a reasonable notice period and a fee, especially with a letter from your employer confirming the transfer.
2. **Ask your employer.** Relocation packages often cover lease-break costs.
3. **Find a replacement tenant.** This often reduces or eliminates what you owe.
4. **Know the mitigation rule.** In many places, including most US states, a landlord must make reasonable efforts to re-rent the unit rather than charge you the full remaining rent.

If you just walk away, you can be liable for the remaining rent, lose your deposit, and take a hit to your credit or rental history.
