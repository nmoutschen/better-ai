<!-- prompt: I wake up at 4am most nights and can't fall back asleep. Why does this happen? -->
Waking around 4am and not being able to get back to sleep is very common. It usually comes from a few overlapping causes.

**Why 4am specifically**
- **Sleep architecture:** Deep sleep is concentrated in the first half of the night. By 4am you're mostly in lighter REM and light sleep, so you wake more easily.
- **Circadian rhythm:** Body temperature bottoms out around 4-5am, and cortisol begins rising before waking. If your stress system is overactive, that rise can pull you fully awake.
- **Falling sleep pressure:** After ~6-7 hours, the buildup of adenosine (your sleep drive) has mostly been used up, so there's less to keep you under.

**Common triggers**
- **Stress and anxiety:** Probably the most common cause. Elevated cortisol and a racing mind make it hard to drift back off. Early-morning waking is also a classic sign of depression.
- **Alcohol:** It helps you fall asleep but fragments sleep in the second half of the night, as it wears off.
- **Caffeine:** Its half-life is 5-6 hours, so afternoon coffee can still affect you.
- **Blood sugar dips:** A large late meal or sugary evening snack can cause a drop and a stress-hormone response overnight.
- **Bedroom factors:** Heat, light, noise, or a partner or pet disturbing you.
- **Medical causes:** Sleep apnea, acid reflux, needing to urinate (enlarged prostate, diabetes), pain, thyroid problems, and perimenopause/menopause (night sweats, hormonal shifts).
- **Medications:** Some antidepressants, steroids, and stimulants can do this.
- **Irregular schedule or napping:** Both weaken the sleep drive.

**What helps**
- Keep a consistent wake time, and get bright light within an hour of waking.
- Cut caffeine after noon and limit alcohol, especially within 3-4 hours of bed.
- Keep the room cool, dark, and quiet.
- Don't watch the clock. Checking it fuels anxiety.
- If you're awake for ~20 minutes, get up, keep the lights low, and do something calm until you're sleepy. Lying there frustrated trains your brain to associate bed with wakefulness.
- Try a slow-breathing or body-scan exercise if you stay in bed.
- Write down worries or a to-do list before bed so your mind isn't rehearsing them at 4am.

**When to see a doctor**
- It's happened most nights for 3+ months (that meets the definition of chronic insomnia).
- You snore loudly, gasp, or wake unrefreshed (possible sleep apnea).
- You feel persistently low, hopeless, or anxious.
- It's affecting your daytime function.

The first-line treatment for chronic insomnia is **CBT-I** (cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia). It's more effective long-term than sleeping pills and is available through apps and online programs as well as therapists.
