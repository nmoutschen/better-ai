# Mandara River Volunteers: 2026 in review

This year we met for 22 clean-up mornings between February and November, two more than in 2025. Together, members gave
1,340 volunteer hours and removed 4.8 tonnes of waste from the riverbanks between Pallewela bridge and the old ferry
landing. About 61% of that waste, by weight, was plastic, most of it bottles and food packaging washed down after the
monsoon rains in May and June.

Membership grew from 176 to 214, with 38 new members joining over the year. Many of them came through our new
partnership with three schools in the district: Sri Sumangala College, St Anne's Girls' School and Pallewela Central
College. Each school brought a class of older students to at least two clean-ups, and the students at St Anne's ran their
own count of the plastic they collected, which they presented to the district council in September.

In June we opened the sorting station at Pallewela bridge, paid for by a grant of 450,000 rupees from the district
council. Waste is now sorted on the day it is collected, and the recycling company in Wennappuwa collects the plastic and
metal every two weeks. In the five months since it opened, 1.1 tonnes of the waste we collected went to recycling instead
of the landfill.

Since March we have also worked with the environmental science department of the regional university, whose students
test the water at five points along our stretch of the river every month. At two of the five points, the level of
dissolved oxygen, which fish and other river life need, rose between March and October. The university says one year of
data is not enough to show a trend, and the testing will continue in 2027.

Our finances are in good order. Income for the year was 612,000 rupees, including the council grant, membership fees and
a fundraising dinner in August that raised 84,000 rupees. We spent 548,000 rupees, mostly on the sorting station,
gloves, bags and transport, which leaves a reserve of 64,000 rupees. The full accounts will be presented at the annual
general meeting.

At the last clean-up in November, members chose Anjali Perera and Joseph Fernando as volunteers of the year. Anjali
organised the school partnership, and Joseph has driven the transport van to every clean-up since 2023.

For 2027 the committee proposes 25 clean-ups, a second sorting station at the ferry landing, and a water-testing training
day so that members can take part in the monthly testing. These plans depend on a new grant, which we will apply for in
February.

The annual general meeting will be held on Saturday 23 January 2027 at 10:00 in the Pallewela community hall. Two
committee roles are open: treasurer, as Mei Ling Wong steps down after four years, and safety lead. Please send
